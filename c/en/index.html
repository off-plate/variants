Most of your repetitive work does not need AI

Most of the repetitive work in a company does not need AI. A file that moves from one folder to another, a report assembled from the same three sources every Monday, a confirmation email that is always the same six lines. Plain automation handles that well, and it is cheaper to build and cheaper to keep running. A model is worth paying for when a machine has to read something a person wrote, decide between options that are not fixed in advance, or draft text that someone will send to a customer.

So we start with how your business actually runs. We go through the recurring tasks one by one and give each of them one of three verdicts: you need nothing, plain automation is enough, or this genuinely needs AI. We build only the third kind. The written breakdown, what we call the rozpis in Czech, is free and comes after the first call.

The three verdicts

  • You need nothing. The task happens a few times a month, or the rule behind it changes so often that keeping an automation alive would cost more than doing the work by hand. We say so and move on.
  • Plain automation is enough. The steps are fixed and the data is already structured. This is a scenario built in the tools you have, with no model in the loop and nothing metered per use.
  • This genuinely needs AI. Somewhere in the process there is unstructured text, speech or a scan, and the next step depends on reading it. A supplier invoice in a layout nobody has seen before. A voice note from a site. That is where a model earns its cost.

Where we usually start

  • Construction firms. Extra work is dictated on site as it happens, matched back to the right job, and compared with the quote. The difference shows up before you invoice instead of after.
  • E-shops. Margin measured against the real purchase price. That price sits in supplier invoices as PDFs, not in the shop, so we read the invoices and put the actual cost next to each product in Shoptet (the Czech e-commerce platform most local shops run on) or whichever platform you use.
  • Accounting offices. Incoming documents are read and pre-posted, so the předkontace (the pre-posting step, where accounts are assigned to a document before it is booked) is prepared for you. Anything the system is unsure about is flagged for a person instead of guessed.
  • Vehicle workshops. A mechanic dictates what they found, on the car in front of them, including items noted during an STK inspection (the mandatory Czech roadworthiness test). The customer-facing write-up prepares itself from that.

What it costs

  • One automation: 12,000 CZK, one off.
  • One process: 25,000 to 60,000 CZK to build, plus 4,000 to 8,000 CZK a month to run it.

These figures are indicative, not final. They give you the order of size before we talk. The real number for your case is in the rozpis, and you get that before you commit to anything.

What we clear up before you ask

  • You own what we build. Scenarios, prompts, access, documentation. All of it lives in your accounts and is handed over to you.
  • If you stop paying, we stop watching and fixing. What is built keeps running until something breaks. Then it stays broken until someone fixes it.
  • Your data sits in Czechia or the EU. The zpracovatelská smlouva (the Czech data processing agreement required under GDPR) is signed before we touch anything.
  • AI running costs are metered per use. We write a monthly cap into the contract, so the bill cannot surprise you.
  • A person at your company signs off every decision. The system prepares the work and flags what is uncertain. A named human approves it.
  • We have no public reference yet. We will not pretend otherwise.
